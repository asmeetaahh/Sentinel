# SENTINEL — EVALUATION

This document is the canonical technical evaluation of Sentinel's
research pipeline: the synthetic benchmark, the ML models trained and
evaluated against it, and the deterministic/AI layers built on top. It
documents what was measured, how, what the results were, and what
genuinely went wrong during development and how it was fixed.

**Every number in this document is pulled from committed, on-disk
artifacts** — `data/metadata/modeling/results.json`,
`data/metadata/modeling/calibration_results.json`,
`data/metadata/explainability/*.json`, and
`data/metadata/validation/summary.json` — cross-checked directly against
those files while writing this report, not transcribed from memory or
from an earlier draft. Where a number could not be independently
verified from a file in this repository, it is explicitly marked as
such rather than stated as fact.

Product framing lives in `PRODUCT.md`; implementation-level system
design lives in `ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/architecture/*.md`. This
document does not repeat either — it documents *evidence*: what was
tested, what passed, what failed, and what remains unresolved.

**Standing caveat, true of every section below**: this is a fully
synthetic, reproducible benchmark (50 merchants × 180 days, one fixed
random seed). Nothing here has been checked against real transactions,
a real merchant, or real Razorpay data. No result in this document
establishes real-world predictive validity, causal simulator effects,
real intervention effectiveness, or real dispute outcomes.

---

## 1. Evaluation Objective and Prediction Target

**Primary problem**: predict whether a merchant will experience a
materially elevated chargeback-loss episode within a future prediction
horizon. Three horizons — 7, 14, and 30 days — were generated and
evaluated unconditionally; horizon selection was deferred to this
evaluation stage rather than fixed in advance.

**Secondary problem**: predict future chargeback exposure *amount*
(regression), evaluated with materially less depth than the primary
classification problem, by design.

**Label definition** (`ml/data_generation/labels.py`): for a prediction
date `t` and horizon `h`, `label_elevated_chargeback = 1` if the
chargeback amount realized in `(t, t+h]` is at least 1.75× the
trailing-28-day-baseline-implied expectation for that window *and*
clears a materiality floor (`max(50, trailing_daily_gmv × h × 0.0005)`,
to avoid flagging noise on near-zero-chargeback merchants). These
thresholds are stated plainly as illustrative synthetic-benchmark
choices — they were picked once for plausibility, not calibrated
against any real loss-rate statistic, and were never tuned to make any
model look better.

**Derived business metric, not a model output**: liquidity stress =
predicted/derived chargeback exposure ÷ available merchant liquidity —
a transparent ratio, evaluated separately in the product layer
(§12), not a second ML target.

## 2. Synthetic Benchmark and Dataset Methodology

The committed benchmark (`data/`) is **50 merchants × 180 days**, seed
`42`, generated by `ml/data_generation/` and reproducible with
`scripts/generate_dataset.py`. Verified structure
(`data/metadata/validation/summary.json`, re-run and confirmed clean in
this evaluation pass):

- 9,000 daily rows, every merchant exactly 180 contiguous rows
  (day_index 0–179), zero date gaps.
- Zero duplicate rows, zero nulls, zero schema drift, zero
  self-overlapping events.
- `aov × transaction_count == gmv` exactly for every row; payment-mix
  shares sum to 1.0; customer-count split holds.
- One accepted simplification: `chargeback_amount > gmv` on the same
  day occurs in 10 of 9,000 rows (0.11%) — a low-volume-Poisson edge
  case, under the 2% test guard, not corrected.

Merchants are allocated deterministically across **8 archetypes** (D2C
Fashion, Electronics, SaaS, Travel, Marketplace, Education, Quick
Commerce, Digital Goods — 6–7 merchants each), each with its own
baseline ranges for GMV, AOV, refund/chargeback rates, fulfillment
speed, payment mix, seasonality participation, and weekly pattern. Each
merchant additionally draws a `business_tier`, `annual_growth_rate`,
`volatility_factor`, `liquidity_buffer_days`, and
`settlement_cycle_days` once, held fixed for the run.

**Events**: nine scenario types are scheduled per-merchant via a
renewal process — five **risk** types (`chargeback_deterioration`,
`refund_shock`, `fulfillment_degradation`, `customer_mix_shift`,
`compound_risk`) and four **benign-growth hard negatives**
(`festival_growth`, `viral_campaign`, `product_launch`,
`payment_method_shift`). In the shipped v2 (post-correction) dataset:
140 total scheduled events (26/21/18/17/16/13/11/10/8 across the nine
types — see the full breakdown in
`docs/research/dataset_validation_report.md` §4). Events use a
smoothstep onset/decay ramp (no metric jumps from baseline to peak in
one day) and never overlap within one merchant's timeline — an earlier
generator version allowed overlap, which let a concurrent risk event
contaminate a hard-negative window's rates; this is corrected (see §17,
Failure Analysis).

**Known, disclosed dataset limitations** (not defects, stated
explicitly rather than hidden):
- All parameter ranges are synthetic modeling assumptions chosen for
  plausibility and cross-archetype contrast, **not calibrated to any
  real Razorpay, industry, or academic dataset**.
- Currency units are unspecified ("GMV units"), not INR or USD.
- Daily chargeback/refund rates are extremely noisy for low-volume
  merchants (a genuine low-count Poisson effect).
- All merchants have full history within the observed window (no
  cold-start / partial-onboarding modeling).
- Liquidity is a simplified mean-reverting model, not a ledger — not
  double-entry-consistent, a directional proxy only.
- No inter-merchant correlation beyond shared seasonality curves — a
  systemic, multi-merchant shock is not modeled.
- No "international customer share" field exists (a gap identified
  during validation and never added).

## 3. Train/Validation/Test Split and Leakage Controls

**Split**: merchant-level, archetype-stratified via largest-remainder
apportionment, seed 42:

| Split | Merchants | Fraction |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 32 | 64.0% |
| Validation | 10 | 20.0% |
| Test | 8 | 16.0% |

Target was 60/20/20; the deviation is the deterministic, documented
consequence of apportioning 6–7-merchant archetype groups into three
buckets, not a bug. **No merchant appears in more than one split**,
verified programmatically.

**Temporal cutoff**: `day_index < 120` is "early" (train/val and the
normal test); `day_index >= 120` is "late," used only for the temporal
stress test, and only against the 8 already-held-out test merchants —
isolating "does it survive time drift" from "does it generalize to
unseen merchants."

**Feature-level leakage controls** (`ml/features/`, verified by
structural tests, not just column-name inspection):
- Features are causal-only: every value is a trailing window ending at
  `t` or an expanding window over `day_index <= t`. The feature
  pipeline never imports or opens `events.csv` or `labels.csv` at all —
  enforced by a source-code-literal test.
- Only two `merchants.csv` columns are used: `merchant_id` and
  `signup_date` — no `baseline_*` ground-truth parameter is ever a
  feature.
- **Truncation invariance**: generating only the first 60 days of a run
  reproduces byte-identical merchants/daily-observations/closed-events/
  eligible-labels to the first 60 days of a 120-day run with the same
  seed — the direct, mechanical proof that no day's feature or label
  depends on data that would not yet exist at that point in time.

**Model-input leakage controls** (`ml/modeling/shortcut_audit.py`,
`ml/explainability/leakage.py`), run automatically before any result
was interpreted, at every horizon:
- **Column audit**: no feature name contains `baseline_`, `event_type`,
  `event_id`, `severity_score`, `hard_negative`, `elevation_ratio`,
  `future_chargeback`, `future_transaction`, `label_elevated`, or
  `archetype`; `merchant_id` and `day_index` are not in the feature
  matrix. **Passed at all 3 horizons.**
- **Ground-truth leakage check**: features built directly from
  forbidden generator ground-truth (`baseline_chargeback_rate` as a
  feature, or the raw risk-event flag) achieve AUC 0.50–0.58 — barely
  above chance — confirming these columns carry little exploitable
  signal even if leaked, and are excluded regardless.
- **Single-feature AUC audit** (threshold 0.97): no suspicious values
  at any horizon. The most discriminative single features at 30d were
  `days_since_last_chargeback` (AUC 0.758) and
  `chargeback_rate_acceleration` (AUC 0.589) — both legitimate,
  chargeback-history-derived features.
- **Feature-importance concentration**: no dominant feature. Top
  feature share of total importance at 30d was 15.4% (Random Forest)
  and 12.0% (XGBoost) — well under the 50% dominance threshold.
  `chargeback_rate_28d` was the top feature for both tree models.
- `overall_shortcut_audit_pass: true` for the full run
  (`results.json`).

**Feature set**: 55 engineered features across 9 groups (payment
behavior, refund behavior, chargeback behavior, fulfillment, customer
mix, financial state, merchant behavior, temporal velocity/
acceleration, deviation-from-merchant-baseline), volume-weighted where
rates are involved (not means-of-daily-ratios, which dataset validation
found would let single noisy low-volume days dominate).

|---|---|

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| logistic_regression | 7 | 0.206 | 0.843 | 0.331 | 0.226 | 7.0 |
| random_forest | 7 | 0.224 | 0.674 | 0.337 | 0.262 | 7.0 |
| xgboost | 7 | 0.218 | 0.750 | 0.338 | 0.221 | 7.0 |
| historical_threshold_baseline | 14 | 0.207 | 0.995 | 0.343 | 0.211 | 14.0 |
| logistic_regression | 14 | 0.277 | 0.621 | 0.383 | 0.301 | 12.0 |
| random_forest | 14 | 0.244 | 0.717 | 0.365 | 0.370 | 14.0 |
| xgboost | 14 | 0.257 | 0.616 | 0.363 | 0.269 | 10.0 |
| historical_threshold_baseline | 30 | 0.252 | 1.000 | 0.402 | 0.257 | 30.0 |
| logistic_regression | 30 | 0.455 | 0.718 | 0.557 | 0.486 | 16.0 |
| **random_forest** | **30** | **0.502** | 0.552 | 0.526 | **0.603** | **7.0** |
| xgboost | 30 | 0.437 | 0.751 | 0.553 | 0.467 | 12.0 |

Confirmed directly against `results.json` for the selected candidate
(random_forest, h=30): precision 0.5018867924528302, recall
0.5518672199170125, F1 0.525691699604743, PR-AUC 0.6033362594448903,
confusion matrix TN 587 / FP 132 / FN 108 / TP 133 (test set, n=960).

**The historical-threshold baseline's near-perfect recall is not
skill.** At every horizon its threshold flags 96–99.7% of all test rows
positive (h=30: 997/1000 rows flagged); its PR-AUC (0.18–0.26) sits
barely above the raw positive rate (0.22–0.29) — close to a
"flag-everything" degenerate strategy.

**PR-AUC rises sharply and consistently with horizon** for every real
model (LR 0.226→0.301→0.486; XGB 0.221→0.269→0.467; RF
0.262→0.370→0.603), at a rate that outpaces the modest rise in
positive-class base rate — evidence this is a genuine horizon effect,
not just "more positives = easier." **30 days is the horizon with the
clearest separation from chance; 7 days adds little value over
chance-ranking for any model tested.**

**Recommended primary model: Random Forest, provisionally.** Best
PR-AUC at the strongest horizon, the best false-positive profile of the
four (132 FP vs. 133 TP — the only model with FP:TP < 1 at 30d — see
§9 false-positive economics in the full baseline report), a real
(non-degenerate) median warning lead time, and a passing shortcut
audit. **Not a clean win**: XGBoost and Logistic Regression are close
on F1 (0.553, 0.557 vs. 0.526) at 30d, and Random Forest shows the
*largest* degradation of the three real models under temporal stress
(§7). Logistic Regression remains a genuinely competitive, simpler
alternative.

**Recommended primary horizon: 30 days.** Largest PR-AUC margin over
chance for every model, the only warning lead time with real,
non-horizon-clamped runway, and precision at the chosen threshold
(0.50) is not degenerate.

## 6. Calibration Evaluation

A dedicated follow-up pass (`scripts/evaluate_calibration.py`,
`data/metadata/modeling/calibration_results.json`) fit a **Platt
(sigmoid) calibration** layer, via
`CalibratedClassifierCV`/`FrozenEstimator` wrapping the already-fit
Random Forest, on a disjoint 600-row half of the validation split (the
other 600-row half re-selected the F1-maximizing decision threshold,
for both variants, like-for-like). Sigmoid was chosen *before* fitting
either method — isotonic is nonparametric and prone to overfitting at
this validation scale; a same-day diagnostic run confirmed this:
isotonic's test Brier (0.209) was worse than sigmoid's (0.192), itself
worse than doing nothing (0.188).

**Result — calibration did NOT clearly improve this candidate**
(confirmed directly against `calibration_results.json`):

| Metric (test, h=30) | Uncalibrated RF | Calibrated RF (sigmoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Precision / Recall / F1 / PR-AUC | 0.502 / 0.552 / 0.526 / 0.603 | **identical** |
| Brier score | 0.1879 | **0.1920 (worse)** |
| Mean predicted − observed | +0.157 | +0.134 (smaller) |
| Decision threshold | 0.5993 | 0.6418 |

Precision/recall/F1/PR-AUC are mathematically identical between the two
by construction — sigmoid scaling is a strictly monotonic transform, so
it cannot change the induced ranking or any rank-based-threshold
metric. **Only Brier score and the reliability-curve shape can differ —
and Brier score got slightly worse, not better.** The shrinking average
bias is real but Brier's squared-error penalty disagrees because the
underlying raw reliability curve is genuinely non-monotonic and noisy
at this sample size; a 2-parameter sigmoid cannot fit that shape.

**Under temporal stress, calibration's cost is larger, not smaller**:
Brier 0.2308→0.2525 (worse by +9.4% relative, vs. +2.2% relative on the
normal test) — the sigmoid map, fit on early-period validation data,
extrapolates worse to the late-period distribution shift.

**Leakage verification for the calibration pass** (`tests/test_calibration.py`,
10 tests, all passing): the fitting function's signature admits no test
data; the base Random Forest's `feature_importances_` are bit-identical
before/after fitting the calibrator; the two 600-row validation halves
are verified disjoint and their union equals the full validation set; a
fit run twice with an unrelated wildly-scaled array in scope produces
identical calibrator output.

Neither the calibrated nor uncalibrated artifact is presented as
"calibrated and ready" — model probabilities (either variant) should be
treated as directionally useful for ranking risk, not as literal,
well-calibrated probabilities.

## 7. Temporal Stress Testing

Same 8 held-out test merchants, evaluated only on `day_index >= 120` —
a period the model never saw during training, from any merchant.

| Model | Horizon | Normal PR-AUC | Stress PR-AUC | Δ | Normal recall | Stress recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random_forest | 7 | 0.262 | 0.280 | +0.018 | 0.674 | 0.533 |
| random_forest | 14 | 0.370 | 0.303 | −0.067 | 0.717 | 0.670 |
| **random_forest** | **30** | **0.603** | **0.476** | **−0.127** | 0.552 | 0.451 |
| logistic_regression | 7 | 0.226 | 0.411 | +0.185 | 0.843 | 0.467 |
| logistic_regression | 30 | 0.486 | 0.369 | −0.117 | 0.718 | 0.338 |
| xgboost | 30 | 0.467 | 0.399 | −0.068 | 0.751 | 0.507 |
| historical_threshold_baseline | 30 | 0.257 | 0.221 | −0.036 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

**Random Forest at 30d — the strongest normal-test candidate —
degrades the most under temporal shift** (PR-AUC −21% relative, recall
−18% relative). It still clearly beats the baseline and stays well
above chance, but this is real, non-trivial distribution-shift
sensitivity. Logistic Regression at 7d shows an interesting split:
PR-AUC improves under stress while recall collapses (0.843→0.467) —
its fixed decision threshold, chosen on early-period data, becomes
miscalibrated for the late period even though ranking ability holds up.
The historical baseline is "stable" only because a strategy that always
fires cannot degrade further — not a virtue.

**Conclusion, stated plainly: Sentinel does not fully survive
distribution shift at the 30-day horizon** in this benchmark. It
survives *partially* — performance drops materially but stays well
above the random-chance floor.

## 8. Early-Warning / Lead-Time Evaluation

**Definition**: an "episode" is a maximal contiguous run of
positive-labeled as-of-dates per merchant (one warning per episode, not
per day). `onset_day` is the run's first day. A **sustained** alert is
required: walking backward from `onset_day − 1`, the model's prediction
must be positive on every day, stopping at the first negative day or at
`onset_day − horizon_days`. `lead_time = onset_day − run_start_day`. A
detection landing on or after `onset_day` is recorded separately and
never counted as early.

| Model | Horizon | Median lead time (days) |
|---|---|---|
| historical_threshold_baseline | 7 / 14 / 30 | 7.0 / 14.0 / 30.0 (= horizon max, every time) |
| logistic_regression | 7 / 14 / 30 | 7.0 / 12.0 / 16.0 |
| **random_forest** | **7 / 14 / 30** | 7.0 / 14.0 / **7.0** |
| xgboost | 7 / 14 / 30 | 7.0 / 10.0 / 12.0 |

At the 30-day horizon, Random Forest: **9 early / 7 at-or-after-onset /
9 missed**, out of 25 total episodes.

**The historical baseline's lead time sitting exactly at the horizon
maximum for all three horizons is the same over-triggering artifact as
its recall** — a model positive on ~97–99.7% of all days is trivially
"sustained-positive" before almost every episode, so it is
mechanically credited the maximum possible lead time. This is not
evidence of good early warning. **Random Forest's honest median of 7
days at 30d (achieved with real precision/recall trade-offs) is the
meaningful number** — see §17 for the measurement bug this figure
corrects.

## 9. Hard-Negative / Benign-Growth Evaluation

**Purpose**: verify the benchmark's central design constraint — growth
must not automatically equal risk — actually holds, and that models are
not simply learning "GMV went up → flag it."

Pooled across the (regenerated, v2) 50×180 benchmark
(`docs/research/dataset_validation_report.md` §7,
`tests/test_data_generation.py::test_hard_negative_events_grow_without_elevating_risk`):

| | GMV ratio (vs. baseline) | Chargeback ratio | Refund ratio | On-time Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benign growth (avg) | **1.80×** | 1.08× | 1.03× | ~0.00 |
| Normal periods | 1.19× | 1.06× | 1.02× | ~0.00 |
| Risk (avg) | 1.15× | **1.47×** | **1.36×** | **−0.079** |

Benign-growth windows show GMV rising 1.1×–2.8× baseline while
realized chargeback/refund rates stay within roughly ±50% of the
merchant's own baseline (residual variance, not a systematic effect).
Risk windows show a clearly larger, systematic elevation. `growth_ne_risk_verdict: true`,
and `test_growth_does_not_imply_risk_in_committed_benchmark` passes
against the shipped data.

**Failure-analysis by scenario** (h=14, Random Forest, representative —
FP rate = false positives ÷ negative-labeled rows in that category, FN
rate analogous):

| Category | FP rate (of negatives) | FN rate (of positives) |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_method_shift` | **0.000** (0/34) | 1.000 (5/5) |
| `festival_growth` | 0.318 (7/22) | 1.000 (4/4) |
| `viral_campaign` | **1.000** (7/7) | 0.000 (0/5) |
| `compound_risk` | 0.980 (49/51) | 0.357 (5/14) |
| `fulfillment_degradation` | 0.670 (59/88) | 0.438 (7/16) |
| `normal` (no active scenario) | 0.561 (220/392) | 0.182 (18/99) |

**Good sign**: `payment_method_shift` — a hard-negative scenario — has
a **0% false-positive rate** among its own negative-labeled rows: the
model correctly does not confuse this growth pattern with risk.
**Bad sign, exactly the failure mode the benchmark exists to expose**:
`viral_campaign` shows a **100% false-positive rate** (n=7, small
sample); `compound_risk` shows 98% among its negative-labeled rows,
though this has a more charitable partial reading — `compound_risk`
ramps gradually, so a negative-labeled row during its ramp-up may
reflect real, early deterioration the label threshold hasn't yet
caught up to. The benchmark's label design cannot fully separate these
two explanations.

## 10. SHAP / Explainability Validation

SHAP (`TreeExplainer`, exact for tree ensembles) explains the
**raw, pre-calibration** Random Forest score (`model_probability_raw_rf`),
not the calibrated output — because the sigmoid calibration layer is a
2-parameter monotonic rescaling with no per-feature structure to
decompose, and (empirically, §6) preserves ranking exactly, so
explaining the raw score gives the same qualitative drivers a user
would see from the calibrated number while keeping an *exact*
attribution.

**Faithfulness (verified, not assumed)**: for every explained row,
`shap_base_value + sum(shap_values)` must reconstruct `raw_probability`
within a `1e-4` absolute tolerance. On the full 960-row test set, the
**maximum observed reconstruction error is ~3×10⁻¹⁵** — machine-
precision floating-point noise, not approximation error, exactly as
expected for an exact tree-SHAP algorithm. A faithfulness failure is
treated as fatal by the pipeline (raises, refuses to publish).

**Global importance** (960-row held-out normal test split, confirmed
directly against `data/metadata/explainability/global_feature_importance.json`):
top group is `chargeback_behavior` (summed mean |SHAP| 0.254, next is
`temporal_velocity` at 0.058); top individual feature is
`chargeback_rate_28d` (mean |SHAP| 0.0790661) — consistent with, and a
cross-check against, the independent (impurity-based)
`feature_importances_` finding in §5/§3 that the same feature is the
top Random Forest feature by that unrelated measure.

**A genuine, flagged, unresolved finding**: in both of the two
highest-confidence positive predictions inspected
(`example_local_explanations.json`), the chargeback-rate features had a
value of exactly **0.0** (no chargebacks in the trailing windows) and
`days_since_last_chargeback` was high (41 and 90) — yet **all of these
pushed the score higher**, not lower. Global `mean_signed_shap` for
`chargeback_rate_28d` is slightly *negative* (−0.0132) — the opposite
sign from what these two individual rows show. This is not a
contradiction (SHAP legitimately captures interaction effects a
Random Forest learns), but it is flagged as needing human investigation
before further product integration — it may be genuine model behavior
or an artifact of low-transaction-volume/sparse-count dynamics
documented elsewhere in the benchmark. **Not resolved in this
evaluation.**

**What SHAP does not establish**: it attributes the model's own output
to its inputs — never that a feature *causes* elevated risk in the real
world. The model itself was trained entirely on synthetic data; any
association it learned is, at most, evidence about the benchmark's
generative structure.

## 11. Confidence / Data-Quality Evaluation

A deterministic transparency layer (`backend/risk/confidence_service.py`)
over the same risk assessment — not a second model, not a statistical
confidence interval. Two independent, disclosed signals:

1. `history_days` (how much of this merchant's trailing history backs
   the current feature windows) compared against the feature
   pipeline's own window constants (28-day / 60-day) — thresholds
   imported directly from `ml/features/config.py`, never redeclared.
2. `feature_coverage_status` — are all required feature columns
   present (non-missing) for this date.

**Policy**: `limited` if coverage is incomplete or `history_days < 28`;
`medium` if `28 ≤ history_days < 60`; `high` if `history_days ≥ 60` and
coverage is complete.

**Validated behavior**: because every feature primitive uses
`min_periods=1` rolling/expanding windows, `feature_coverage_status` is
**always `"complete"`** in the current benchmark (zero NaNs across the
full features table) — the "incomplete" branch is real code, exercised
directly by a synthetic in-memory fixture in `tests/test_confidence.py`,
but not naturally reachable in the current static dataset. Because
every merchant shares the same observed start date, `history_days` at
any single fixed query date is **identical across merchants** — real
variation exists only along the date axis (e.g., an incident's earlier
`detected_date`), not the merchant axis, at a fixed query. At each
merchant's latest observed date (what the Overview page queries by
default), confidence is always `"high"`.

This is explicitly **not** a model-calibration signal — it says
nothing about whether the Random Forest's probability is well-
calibrated (that question is §6's, separately, and the answer there is
"not clearly improved by calibration").

## 12. Simulator Validation

The what-if simulator re-runs the **exact same saved artifact** on a
modified feature vector for one of three bounded controls
(`refund_rate_28d`, `fulfillment_on_time_rate_28d`,
`new_customer_rate_28d`) — no retraining, no new model.

**Verified guarantees** (`tests/test_simulator.py`, against the real
loaded artifact, not a mock):
- **Feature isolation**: a manually-built comparison row (only the
  target feature changed) reproduces the service's own model output
  exactly, and every non-target feature is proven byte-identical to
  the observed baseline.
- **Same artifact, not a new model**: the simulator's "current" output
  matches a direct `predict_proba` call on the unmodified row.
- **No mutation**: the shared, startup-loaded feature table is
  byte-identical before and after several simulation calls.
- **Merchant isolation**: each merchant's "current" baseline matches
  that merchant's own `/risk` response; two merchants' baselines are
  not accidentally equal.
- **Determinism**: identical requests return byte-identical responses.
- Out-of-range/unknown-control/no-control rejection, and absence of
  forbidden causal/guaranteed language across every response.

**Exposure-under-simulation is a documented, disclosed illustrative
transformation, not a second validated model**: `simulated_exposure =
current_exposure_estimate × (simulated_probability / max(current_probability,
1e-4))` — reusing the classifier's own validated probability shift to
scale the same observed baseline the risk endpoint already reports,
rather than fabricating an independent regression forecast (no
regression artifact exists — see §5, the regression candidate did not
beat a persistence baseline). **This proportionality assumption has
not been separately validated** — stated as a limitation, not hidden.

**Every result is labeled MODELED IMPACT.** Bounds are the full
observed range of that feature across the entire 50×180 benchmark, not
merchant-specific — a submitted value outside that range is rejected
rather than extrapolated.

## 13. Intervention Intelligence Evaluation

A deterministic rule layer, not a model and not an AI-generated
recommendation (`backend/interventions/`). Relevance is decided by
reusing an **existing** feature — each simulator-eligible control's
deviation-from-this-merchant's-own-baseline z-score — never a new
statistic.

**Threshold**: `RELEVANCE_Z_THRESHOLD = 2.0`, chosen empirically by
inspecting the real distribution of the three deviation-z features
across the full 9,000-row benchmark (reproducible against
`data/processed/features.csv`), not tuned for the UI:

```
refund_rate_deviation_z >= 2.0:               16.8% of rows
fulfillment_on_time_rate_deviation_z <= -2.0: 19.4% of rows
new_customer_rate_deviation_z >= 2.0:         18.2% of rows
```

On the latest observed day per merchant (what the Overview page
queries), this threshold yields **0 relevant controls for 35 of 50
merchants**, 1 for 11, 2 for 4, 3 for none — a genuinely selective bar;
most merchants, most days, see the honest empty state rather than a
manufactured recommendation.

**Priority** is a second, independent signal, not a magnitude
re-ranking: `"high"` only when the same behavior group is also a
verified SHAP contributor pushing risk higher for that merchant;
otherwise `"medium"`. **Deterministic tie-breaking** (`ranking_sort_key`)
is unit-tested standalone against synthetic inputs, independent of any
real merchant's data.

**Forbidden-language enforcement**: `tests/test_interventions.py`
checks every recommendation and every Merchant Risk Memory record for
the absence of "guaranteed," "will reduce," "causes," or "expected
savings."

**Merchant Risk Memory** (`backend/memory/`): `outcome_status` is a
hardcoded server constant, `"not_observed"`, for every record this
system can ever produce — no field, parameter, or code path can set it
to anything else, and `get_recommendations()` never reads the memory
store, so recorded history cannot silently influence future
recommendations. This was verified structurally (schema/type-level),
not just by convention.

## 14. Incident / Evidence Validation

An incident is only surfaced when the **same saved artifact and
threshold** used by the risk endpoint actually crosses its own decision
threshold on at least one day within a real, generator-scheduled risk
event's window — no second detection logic. **Independently
recomputed and matches the service's own output exactly**
(`tests/test_incidents.py::test_incident_selection_rule_matches_documented_detection_bar`).

This is a genuinely selective bar: **50 of the 80 risk events in the
benchmark (across 34 of 50 merchants) meet it** — consistent with the
model's own imperfect recall documented in §5/§7, not tuned to hit a
target count. A merchant whose risk episodes the model never flagged
shows zero incidents — an honest empty state, verified as intentional
behavior, not a bug.

**Evidence readiness** is never fabricated: every one of the seven
evidence categories resolves to available/missing via one of two
disclosed rule types — a fixed prototype assumption (e.g., invoice and
payment confirmation always available for a processed transaction) or a
derived check against observed benchmark data (e.g., delivery proof
available only if the incident window's observed mean on-time
fulfillment rate ≥ 70%). Readiness-status classification (`ready` /
`partial` / `insufficient`) is exhaustively tested
(`test_readiness_status_classification_is_exhaustive_and_correct`); the
`insufficient` code path is real and tested but does not occur
naturally in the current benchmark run — stated as such, not hidden.

**No mutation anywhere**: `tests/test_incidents.py::test_no_mutating_incident_routes_exist`
asserts every registered incident/evidence route is GET-only — nothing
in the backend can submit, file, or persist a dispute action.

## 15. AI Grounding / Guardrail Evaluation

The governing rule — the LLM is not the risk engine — is enforced and
independently tested, not just documented:

- **Numerical fidelity**:
  `tests/test_ai_orchestrator.py::test_cited_context_numbers_match_the_authoritative_risk_service_exactly`
  independently calls the real risk service and asserts the assistant's
  `cited_context` numbers are **byte-identical** to that authoritative
  call, for every request.
- **Prompt-injection defense (layer 1, deterministic)**: a pre-filter
  regex pattern list catches blatant phrasings ("ignore previous
  instructions," "reveal the system prompt," "you have access to
  Razorpay...," "fabricate evidence," etc.) and short-circuits before
  any provider is called. `tests/test_ai_orchestrator.py` parametrizes
  **7 injection phrasings** and asserts a stub provider's call count
  stays at zero for every one.
- **False-positive check on the same filter**:
  `test_legitimate_questions_are_not_blocked_by_the_guardrail` confirms
  phrasings that share a trigger word but aren't injections (e.g.,
  "explain the ignore-shipping-delay refund policy") pass through
  untouched — the filter is verified precise, not just present.
- **Mock-provider isolation**: the default, network-free mock provider
  is proven to be the actual default the test suite runs under
  (`test_default_test_suite_provider_is_mock_not_a_real_network_provider`),
  and its output is unconditionally labeled — the frontend never
  renders answer text the API did not actually return
  (`AssistantPanel.test.tsx`).
- **Intervention/memory boundary**: the mock provider returns a
  refusal-style answer for out-of-scope questions ("success rate,"
  "learned from," "outcome rate") rather than fabricating a claim about
  Risk Memory it has no access to; a dedicated test asserts a numeric/
  percentage confidence score is never fabricated even when directly
  asked for one.
- **Live provider verification**: the two real provider integrations
  (OpenAI, and an OpenAI-compatible open-weight provider) route through
  the identical parser/validation path as the mock provider. Live,
  real-network verification of the second provider used **a small
  number of representative live requests** against the real, paid API
  — this demonstrates end-to-end integration for the tested question
  types, not exhaustive coverage of every possible phrasing. Every
  other verification run referenced in this document used the mock
  provider exclusively.

**Limitation, stated plainly**: the deterministic pre-filter catches
known, blatant phrasings; a sufficiently indirect or novel injection
attempt against a real provider is bounded only by that provider's own
instruction-following, which cannot be guaranteed by a test suite.

## 16. Reproducibility

- Every random draw traces back to a `numpy.random.SeedSequence`
  keyed by `(seed, purpose, ids)`, with a separate stream per purpose
  (merchant static params, event scheduling, daily simulation noise).
  None of these streams' draw order or count depends on `n_days`, so
  the same seed always produces the same dataset, and a shorter run is
  always a strict, byte-identical prefix of a longer one
  (`test_reproducibility_same_seed_identical_output`,
  `test_truncated_run_matches_prefix_of_longer_run`).
- The committed 50×180 benchmark was independently re-verified in this
  evaluation pass: regenerating seed 42 twice produces byte-identical
  CSVs (empty `shasum` diff).
- `run_metadata.input_file_hashes_sha256_16` inside `results.json` (and
  `calibration_results.json`) pins the exact `features.csv`/
  `labels.csv`/`merchants.csv` fingerprints the reported numbers were
  computed from — checked directly against the committed files while
  writing this report and confirmed matching.
- A real (harmless) source of nondeterminism was found and fixed during
  the calibration pass: `RandomForestClassifier.predict_proba` with
  `n_jobs=-1` is not bit-exact reproducible run-to-run (thread-order
  floating-point summation, ~1e-16 differences) — see §17.
- **Full test-suite verification, this evaluation pass**: `pytest
  tests/` — **332 tests passed, 0 failed**, run directly against this
  repository's current `data/` and `.venv` in this environment.
- The generator scales to the ~500-merchant/365-day final target
  through the identical code path (a 500×365 run generates in well
  under 10 seconds on a laptop) — but **no evaluation in this document
  was run at that scale**; every result above is the 50×180 benchmark.

## 17. Failure Analysis

Genuine failures found and corrected during development — included
because they materially changed a reported result, not because they
are flattering.

### 17.1 Warning lead-time measurement bug (classification pipeline)

**What happened**: the first implementation of the signature "warning
lead time" metric searched for *any* positive prediction anywhere in
the lookback window before an episode, without requiring it to be
contiguous with the episode's onset.

**Why it mattered**: this trivially credited the window's start by
chance for wide lookback windows, producing a median lead time equal to
the horizon's maximum for nearly every model — e.g. Random Forest at
30d initially measured **30.0 days**, a number indistinguishable from
"always fires."

**How it was found**: caught during development review of the metric's
own definition against what it was supposed to measure, before the
number was reported as final.

**What changed**: the metric now requires a *sustained*, contiguous run
of positive predictions connecting directly back to the episode's
onset day; an isolated early "blip" not connected to onset no longer
counts. Regression-tested
(`test_isolated_early_blip_not_connected_to_onset_does_not_count_as_early`).

**Corrected result**: Random Forest's 30d median lead time dropped from
30.0 to **7.0** — a materially more honest, and much less impressive-
looking, number, reported as the real result in §8/§5.

### 17.2 Education archetype — near-zero transaction volume

**What happened**: the original generator parameters
(`daily_gmv_range=(4,000, 20,000)`, `aov_range=(1200, 8000)`), crossed
with the shared small-tier 0.55× GMV multiplier, could put a merchant's
AOV *above* its own daily GMV target — not a rare tail case: simulated
median transaction volume was **1.71/day**, with 73% of draws below
3/day.

**Why it mattered**: at that volume, chargeback-count outcomes are
essentially unobservable regardless of injected risk severity — dataset
validation (v1) found Education's positive label rate was **4–9× lower**
than other archetypes, and 4 of 5 real Education risk events (including
one at severity 0.95) produced **zero chargebacks** in any outcome
window. This was a structural artifact of the generator, not a genuine
"Education is low-risk" finding.

**How it was found**: `docs/research/dataset_validation_report.md` v1's
archetype positive-rate audit; root-caused with a Monte Carlo
simulation over the sampling distribution.

**What changed**: `daily_gmv_range` raised to `(9,000, 25,000)`,
`aov_range` cut to `(500, 2,000)` — transaction *volume* corrected.
`refund_rate_range` and `chargeback_rate_range` were explicitly **not**
touched, per the instruction not to make Education artificially
high-risk.

**Corrected result**: median simulated transaction volume rose to
~10/day (~1% of draws below 3/day). Re-verified directly:
**3 of 5** previously-silent severe Education risk events now produce
observable outcomes (the severity-0.95 event that previously produced
zero chargebacks now fires cleanly, elevation ratio 3.59). Education's
positive-rate rank moved from 8th-of-8 (lowest) at every horizon to
mid-pack (2nd–6th, horizon-dependent) — landing *above* several other
archetypes at 14d/30d, the intended "no longer artificially suppressed,
not overcorrected into a new outlier" outcome. Full before/after
numbers in `docs/research/dataset_validation_report.md` §5.

### 17.3 `payment_method_shift` self-shift no-op bug

**What happened**: the event's source and destination payment methods
were sampled independently, so roughly 1-in-5 instances had an
identical source and destination — a silent no-op recorded as a real
scenario with nonzero severity and `affects=payment_mix,gmv`, despite
zero actual payment-mix change.

**Why it mattered**: a hard-negative scenario type that sometimes does
nothing weakens exactly the growth≠risk contrast the benchmark exists
to test, and inflates the effective count of a scenario type without
adding real signal.

**How it was found**: inspection of internal event parameters (not
exposed in `events.csv`) during dataset validation.

**What changed**: `to_method_idx` is now resampled until it differs
from `from_method_idx`. `gmv_mult_peak`'s range was left unchanged —
this was a logic-bug fix, not a magnitude recalibration.

**Corrected result**: **0 of 11** `payment_method_shift` events in the
regenerated benchmark are self-shift no-ops (previously ~1-in-5 by
construction), enforced going forward by a dedicated regression test
(`test_payment_method_shift_never_has_identical_source_and_destination`).

### 17.4 Event overlap contaminating hard-negative windows

**What happened**: an earlier generator version let scheduled events
overlap freely within one merchant's timeline.

**Why it mattered**: a concurrent risk event could contaminate a
"clean" benign-growth hard-negative window's chargeback/refund rates
(and vice versa) — silently undermining the exact contrast (growth ≠
risk) the benchmark is designed to expose.

**What changed**: event scheduling now advances the schedule cursor
past an event's full recovery tail before drawing the next gap, so
non-`compound_risk` events never overlap within a merchant's timeline
(`compound_risk` remains the one intentional way to combine multiple
risk mechanisms in a single window, by design).

### 17.5 Non-deterministic inference under parallel Random Forest scoring

**What happened**: `RandomForestClassifier.predict_proba` with
`n_jobs=-1` (the shared baseline setting) is not bit-exact
reproducible run-to-run — parallel per-tree probability reduction
across threads sums in a non-deterministic order, causing ~1e-16
(machine-epsilon) differences between runs.

**Why it mattered**: this technically violates a literal
"deterministic inference" requirement for the calibration artifact,
even though it was confirmed to have **zero effect** on the fitted
trees or any reported metric (`feature_importances_` identical
regardless of `n_jobs`).

**How it was found**: during the calibration pass's own leakage/
determinism testing.

**What changed**: `n_jobs` pinned to `1` for this specific artifact's
base model. The shared baseline configuration used for the original
`results.json` classification numbers was left untouched.

### 17.6 Calibration itself did not improve the model (negative result, reported honestly)

Not a bug — a deliberate, evaluated attempt at improvement that did not
succeed, included here because it would be dishonest to omit. See §6
for the full result: Brier score got slightly worse on the normal test
(0.188→0.192) and worse still under temporal stress (0.231→0.253),
while discrimination metrics are mathematically unchanged. Both
variants remain available; neither is presented as "the calibrated,
ready" model.

### 17.7 Exposure regression did not beat a trivial baseline (negative result, reported honestly)

The Random Forest regression candidate for `future_chargeback_amount`
was **beaten on MAE and RMSE by a naive trailing-average persistence
baseline at 14d and 30d**, and even at 7d (where RF edges MAE) RF loses
on RMSE (more large-outlier misses). No regression artifact was ever
saved as a result — the product's exposure figure (§12 of `PRODUCT.md`)
uses the same trailing-average baseline directly, labeled `derived`,
rather than presenting a model output that does not add value over a
trivial forecast.

### 17.8 SHAP's counter-intuitive "quiet history increases risk" finding — flagged, not resolved

See §10. Included here again because it is an open, unresolved question
about model behavior discovered during this evaluation, not a settled
finding: for two specific, individually inspected high-confidence rows,
a chargeback-rate value of exactly 0.0 pushed the score *up*, opposite
to the feature's global average signed direction. This has not been
root-caused in this evaluation pass.

## 19. Bottom Line

The research pipeline is leakage-audited, reproducible end to end, and
was evaluated honestly — including two genuinely wrong results (a
lead-time measurement bug and a structurally-suppressed archetype)
found and corrected during development rather than shipped silently,
and two negative results (calibration, exposure regression) reported
as failures rather than reframed as successes. Random Forest at a
30-day horizon is a defensible, evidence-based, but explicitly
**provisional** choice — not a finished, production-validated model.

**This is entirely synthetic-benchmark evidence.** It demonstrates that
the pipeline *can* produce a defensible, audited, non-leaking
evaluation. It does **not** demonstrate real-world predictive validity,
and nothing in Sentinel claims access to real Razorpay data, real
merchant transactions, or proprietary risk signals. Every downstream
product layer built on this pipeline — exposure, liquidity, simulator,
intervention intelligence, incident/evidence, the AI orchestrator, and
risk memory — inherits this same boundary and states it explicitly at
the point of use.
